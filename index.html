The chain reaction

Climate affects farms.
Farms affect food.

Heat, changing rainfall and extremes can alter crop growth, water, soil, pests and harvest timing. Effects vary by crop and place.

☼

Heat and growing seasons

Heat can stress flowering, grain filling and livestock comfort. Some cooler areas may gain frost-free days, but longer seasons do not guarantee larger or more nutritious harvests.

≈

Water, soil and extremes

Rainfall shifts, evaporation, drought, floods and storms can harm crops, erode soil, compete for water and delay farm work.

⌁

Pests and crop quality

Warmer conditions can shift pest and disease pressure. Elevated carbon dioxide may help some crops grow in suitable conditions, while heat, water and nutrient limits can offset gains.

↗

From harvest to household

A farm shock can strain storage and transport, affect prices and income, and make nutritious food harder to obtain throughout the year.

Choices that help

Prepare. Prevent. Protect.

Local evidence matters. Actions should fit the place, crop, water supply and needs of the people who depend on the harvest.

01 / PREPARE

Adapt to local risks

Choose suitable resilient crop varieties, diversify crops and planting dates, protect soil moisture, use water carefully and plan with weather information.

02 / PREVENT

Reduce avoidable emissions

Use fertilizer well, consider suitable methane-reducing rice and livestock practices, avoid unnecessary land clearing and use energy efficiently.

03 / PROTECT

Keep food moving

Improve storage and transport, reduce food loss, prepare alternate routes and maintain emergency reserves where possible.
